The Election Trap – Why Voting Feels Like Sleepwalking Through a Legal Nightmare

A simple, everyday example showing why elections lack real legal delegation and produce null acts. Wake up from the sleepwalk.

Hey there, imagine this: You're a regular guy, let's say like Forrest Gump – simple, straightforward, just trying to handle life one step at a time. You've got some property – maybe a house, a bit of land, or a small business – and you decide you need a legal representative to manage it for you. Someone to handle the paperwork, make deals, and keep things running smooth while you're busy living your life.

So, you head to the notary's office. You sit down, explain what you want, hand over your details, and say, "Okay, let's set this up." The notary smiles and says:

"Great! Your legal representative is already picked out. You can go home now, all good!"

You blink, confused. "Wait, what? Don't I get a big folder of papers – like 20 or 30 pages – that I sign with this representative? Stuff about their duties, my rights, what happens if they mess up or go beyond what I told them? Penalties, protections, all that?"

The notary chuckles. "Nah, don't worry about it! We handle everything here. No need for all that fuss. Just relax, and in 4, 5, or maybe 6 years, we'll check in. If you don't like how it's going, you can pick a different representative then. But until that time? You don't have to do a thing. Sit back and trust us!"

Your jaw drops. This sounds crazy, right? On your own property – stuff you own, that matters to you – you'd never accept this. You'd demand guarantees, rules, accountability. You'd say:

"No way! I have rights over my things, and I'm not getting shut out of the power to control them!"

You'd walk out and find a real notary who does it properly, with contracts, signatures, and safeguards.

But guess what? This is exactly what you accept every time you vote in an election.

It's like you're sleepwalking through the whole thing – moving like a zombie, not really seeing or understanding what's around you. You show up, mark a box, and hand over massive power to "representatives" without any real legal paperwork, no clear duties, no penalties if they screw up, and no way for you to step in if things go wrong. And then? You wait years for the next chance to "pick" again, while they do whatever they want with the country's laws, money, and future – which is basically your sovereignty, your power as a citizen.

Step 1: What Happens in Real Legal Delegation?

In everyday life, when you give someone power over your stuff (like that property example), the law demands a proper process. It's not optional – it's required to make it legit. Here's what it looks like:

  • A contract: A written agreement spelling out exactly what the representative can and can't do.
  • Your signature: You agree to it, knowingly.
  • Their obligations: They promise to act in your best interest, or face consequences like fines or losing the job.
  • Protections for you: If they steal, lie, or mess up, you can sue, fire them, or get your money back.
  • Oversight: You can check in anytime, not just every few years.

Without this? The whole deal is junk. Legally null and void. A judge would throw it out and say, "This never happened – no real delegation here."

Step 2: Elections – The Big Sleepwalk (With Legal Nullity Exposed)

When you vote, you're supposed to be "delegating" your power as a citizen to politicians. But does it follow any of those real legal steps? Nope. Here's the weird parallel:

  • You "pick" a representative (or party) from a list someone else made.
  • No contract: Nothing signed saying what they must do, or what happens if they break promises.
  • No obligations: They can change their mind, take bribes, or ignore you – no automatic penalties.
  • No protections: If they pass bad laws or waste money, you can't sue or fire them mid-term. Just wait for the next election.
  • No oversight: You're shut out. They decide behind closed doors, and you "trust" the system.

It's like that notary scam, but on steroids. You're giving away control over your country's future – taxes, wars, schools, everything – without a shred of real legal backing. And the system acts like it's normal!

Now, let's dig into the legal nullity part – the reason why this is all invalid in civil-law terms. In most legal systems (including civil law traditions common in Europe and Latin America), a delegation or mandate is null and void if it misses essential elements:

  • Lack of proper form: Delegations of power over important matters require written documents, clear signatures, and specific terms. Without them, the act has no legal existence.
  • No true consent: Consent must be informed, voluntary, and specific. A vague ballot mark on a pre-set list isn't informed or specific consent.
  • Missing or indefinite cause/object: The "why" and "what" of the delegation must be clear and defined. In elections, the "mandate" is so vague (no duties, no limits) that it lacks a valid object.
  • Violation of public order or fundamental rights: If the process excludes the principal (the citizen) from real control over their own sovereignty, it violates basic principles and is null from the beginning.

Result: The so-called "delegation" from voter to politician is legally null. And if the delegation is null, then every act based on it (laws, decrees, budgets, treaties) is also null – just like a fake property sale would be voided by a court and all subsequent transactions canceled.

Step 3: Why Do We Accept It? The Sleepwalker Effect

Most people don't notice because it's wrapped in fancy words like "democracy" and "your vote counts." But deep down, it's a trick. You go to the polling booth like an automaton – punch the button, feel good for a minute, then go home and forget. Meanwhile, the real power stays with oligarchs, parties, and institutions that never asked for your real permission.

Think about Forrest Gump again. He ran across the country without knowing why at first – just kept going because it felt right. That's us at elections: We keep "running" the same old system without stopping to ask, "Wait, is this even real?"

The truth is, this setup confiscates your sovereignty. It's not democracy; it's an oligarchy in disguise. And all those government acts? Legally, they're on shaky ground – just like that notary's "deal" would be.

No more sleepwalking. Demand contracts, oversight, and accountability in politics, just like you would for your own property.
